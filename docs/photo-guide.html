Before choosing a label

One flower.
Four separate decisions.

  1. Find fresh, open flowers and check every photo in the observation.
  2. Look at the upper surfaces of at least three petals. Clear needs an obvious ring edge at three exposed bases; Faint needs a visible localized band.
  3. Use a source flower at least 150 pixels across for color and 300 pixels across for the other traits.
  4. Choose Cannot be determined for an unresolved trait. Continue scoring the others independently.

Leave room for uncertainty

A clear limit is
part of a good label.

“Cannot be determined” describes the available evidence. It is different from Low, None, or Absent. A hidden center does not establish an absent ring; a blurred petal does not establish low vein contrast.

If diffuse inner paling cannot be separated into a ring versus broad variation, leave the ring unresolved. Also leave variation unresolved when including or excluding that zone would change its score.

Use the source observation to compare other views. When suitable views disagree, resolve the conflict before assigning a category.
