Protocol v1.1.0 / Source-paper recheck

White and blue need a clearer boundary.

Brinegar’s Figure 1 labels pink A–D, purple E–H, blue I–L, and white M–P. Some blue examples look nearly white; some white examples have conspicuous purple veins. The paper supplies no reproducible white–blue cutoff. Our original “cool tint” interpretation was too confident in two cases, and a recheck found five pale/cool white assignments that also warrant abstention.

Loading withdrawal verification…

These are uncertainty corrections, not reassignment to fit the paper’s map. Geography and current taxon were not used to choose a replacement color. The 100-observation sample also differs from the paper’s 2015–2019 Research Grade image sample, so its color proportions cannot test that map.

01 / The recommendation

Keep the useful distinction.
Hold the finer categories.

These findings describe this reviewer's photographic judgments. They do not demonstrate inherited traits, species boundaries, or population frequencies.

Hold bulk color writes

Petal background

Keep the paper’s four names, but do not force near-white flowers into white or blue. Obtain identifiable author-labeled reference images or clarification and an independent human review. Matching new reviewers to each other alone cannot demonstrate agreement with the paper.

Continue as a pilot

Vein contrast

Both contrast states occurred within white flowers. Faint states also occurred in pink and purple flowers, so contrast adds information beyond background color. Only three faint cases were scored; reliability is still untested.

Vein branching

Mostly longitudinal lines with varying distal Y-forks. Fork visibility and counts differed among petals and with focus. This review did not reveal dependable discrete types; an unresolved fork is not evidence of an unbranched vein.

No routine field yet

Vein color

Purple, pink-purple, and gray/blue-gray appearances were sensitive to exposure, petal background, blur, and reflected light. Paired views could change the appearance substantially. Record exploratory notes, not categorical field values.

04 / What this does and does not establish

The next step is independent review.

The sample contains 50 observations per taxon, selected deterministically from public photo-bearing records with a Flowers annotation, across all quality grades. Each candidate was ranked by SHA-256 of a fixed seed plus its observation ID; the first 50 per taxon were selected. No geographic or color filter was applied. Annotation and taxon-selection biases remain, and unannotated flowers are outside this pilot.

All photos were inspected in observation-level sheets. Selected original-resolution crops were rescaled without sharpening or recoloring. Source flower diameters are approximate manual measurements; color-only flowers below 300 source pixels were never scored for veins. Images were presented without species or location captions, but this was not a fully blinded independent study.

One AI reviewer made the judgments. There is no valid agreement percentage, confusion matrix, or Cohen's kappa from this run. The planned class coverage was not reached: the original review scored only two pink and two blue backgrounds, and three faint-vein cases; the blue assignments were subsequently identified for withdrawal. The proposed 90% agreement / κ ≥ 0.80 gate remains untested. Expand those classes and ask a human reviewer to score the images independently before seeing this audit.

For future branching or hue work, collect detailed, consistently oriented upper-petal photographs of at least three complete petals under neutral illumination, with a color reference for hue. Test repeatability before choosing categories. Vein-hue notes here began partway through the review; they are not a systematic frequency survey.

The owner expressly authorized saving confident pilot fields during review. Existing conflicting labels were not overwritten, and observer restrictions were respected. The website reads actual saved iNaturalist values; these pilot judgments do not validate unrelated community labels.
